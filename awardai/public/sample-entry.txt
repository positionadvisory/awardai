CASE STUDY — "The Quiet Hour" (anonymized sample entry)
Brand: a national quick-service coffee chain (details scrubbed)
Show intent: Spikes Asia — Creative Effectiveness

BACKGROUND & CHALLENGE
Weekday mornings drove the business, but the 2pm-to-5pm window sat almost empty.
Category advertising all fought over the morning rush. The brand had a modest
budget and no new product to launch, only an under-used afternoon and a belief
that people needed a small, unhurried break more than another caffeine hit.

INSIGHT
Afternoons are the loneliest stretch of the working day. Our research with 1,200
office workers found 68% took no real break between lunch and clocking off, and
that those who did reported measurably lower end-of-day stress. The opportunity
was not to sell coffee. It was to give permission to pause.

THE IDEA — "The Quiet Hour"
Every weekday from 2pm to 3pm, participating stores dimmed the lights, muted the
music, and turned off the order-shouting. Staff spoke in lower voices. A second
coffee was half price for anyone who simply sat. We turned the least profitable
hour into the brand's signature.

EXECUTION
- 340 stores adopted The Quiet Hour over eight weeks.
- Out-of-home near office districts ran a single line: "2pm. Breathe. Then coffee."
- A partnership with a meditation app unlocked a free guided three-minute reset,
  redeemable only in-store during the hour.
- No paid social. The story spread through staff and regulars first.

RESULTS (independently measured where noted)
- Afternoon transactions up 41% over the campaign window versus the prior quarter.
- Average afternoon dwell time up from 9 to 23 minutes (in-store observation study).
- Earned media reach of 22 million with zero paid amplification.
- Brand-love score up 14 points among lapsed customers (third-party tracker).
- The half-price second cup lifted, not cut, afternoon revenue per store by 18%.

WHY IT MATTERS
The Quiet Hour did not invent a product or chase the morning fight everyone else
was having. It reframed an empty hour as a cultural gesture, and the commercial
result followed the human one. A small budget, a real insight, a repeatable ritual.
