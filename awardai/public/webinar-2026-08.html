The gap is not talent. It is method.

"Win more" is a wish, not a strategy.

  • You make every client define what they stand for before they spend a dollar. Then awards season comes and you enter on instinct and last year's list.
  • Different shows build different reputations. D&AD craft. Effies and WARC effectiveness. Cannes creative fame. Independent Agency of the Year says something about how you win.
  • Chase all of them and a finite budget produces nothing anyone remembers.

The independents at the top don't enter and hope. They set a number.

"We set agency-wide goals every year. Let's say we set out to win Independent Agency of the Year in Cannes. We identify the number of awards needed, and then we go after it."
Aaron Starkman, Rethink. #2 independent agency, WARC Creative 100.
  • That is not "win more." It is a named title with a number attached, worked backwards.
  • Rethink was named both Independent Agency and Independent Network of the Year at Cannes this year.
"...independence, internationality, and an uncompromisingly creative approach to every single task."
Florian Schill, Serviceplan, on being named Independent Network of the Year, 2022. #1 independent agency, WARC Creative 100.
  • Serviceplan works from the same clarity: name what you stand for, then earn it.

Finish the sentence: "...because ___"

  • The reputation you pick pulls different clients, different talent, different fees. The why is the reason it is worth saying no to the rest.
  • Talent see momentum and stay. Clients read early wins as proof in the next pitch. An acquirer sees a track record, not a claim.
  • If you cannot finish "because ___", that is the real work. And it is due before you open a single entry form.

Four moves that turn the target into entries.

  • Allocate a fixed budget like a portfolio.
  • Climb the ladder: national, then regional, then global.
  • Read what is readable in the jury data.
  • Enter on evidence of odds, not gut.
